# Findings log (raw notes feeding DISAGREEMENTS.md)

Each entry: date, what was found, evidence, status.

- 10/30/2026: GIMBAL true airspeed. Peings and von Rennenkampff (2023) convert IAS 238 to 242 kt at 25,000 ft and -25 C to TAS 363 to 369 kt. The standard subsonic compressible conversion (IAS taken as CAS, ISA pressure at 25,000 ft, temperature -25 C) gives 353.6 to 359.6 kt, about 10 kt lower. Their figure matches roughly 250 KCAS. Evidence: `tests/test_atmosphere.py::test_gimbal_airspeed_is_lower_than_2023_paper`. Status: check the paper's exact conversion and the on-screen Mach reading at M0; effect on the path is a sensitivity run at M2.
- 10/30/2026: GIMBAL azimuth update cadence. The 2023 paper says the displayed azimuth is "updated every other frame". The M1 glyph reader finds the readout changes every 11 to 25 frames (median 17.5), and 34 of 46 clean changes fall on the same phase of a 6-frame cycle (about 5 Hz display update). GOFAST azimuth (23 of 23 changes) and elevation (13 of 14) sit on the same 6-frame phase. Evidence: `data/derived/gimbal/symbology_summary.json`, `data/derived/gofast/symbology_summary.json`. Status: the solver treats each readout as an interval valid until the next 6-frame update, not as a per-frame value.
- 10/30/2026: Frame alignment with Sitrec. Our GIMBAL copy (1031 frames) matches Sitrec's per-frame azimuth 97.3 percent at an offset of -1 frame (Sitrec's changes land 1 to 2 frames earlier). GOFAST: azimuth 99.0 percent at +1, elevation 99.4 percent at 0, range 99.25 percent at 0; the GOFAST offset changes from 1 to 0 near f500, suggesting the copies differ by one dropped or added frame there. Evidence: `data/derived/<case>/symbology_sitrec_check.json`.
- 10/30/2026: GOFAST range first appears at f368 reading 4.4 in our copy; Sitrec's keyframe table carries 4.4 back to f350 (and 5.0 to f0, which is not on screen). Not a disagreement about values, but Sitrec's frame-0 range of 5.0 is a placeholder, not a reading.
- 10/30/2026: Bank angle from the horizon bars agrees with Sitrec's tables to correlation 0.9998 (GIMBAL and GOFAST), but our angles are about 8.5 percent (GIMBAL) and 6.8 percent (GOFAST) larger after a linear fit (rms 0.08 to 0.09 deg after scaling). Candidate cause: pixel aspect ratio (the 640x480 copies may be resampled from a non-square-pixel source), which would distort every angle measured in image space (bank, glare orientation). Status: open, M2 resolves it from the display geometry (the horizon bars and reticle are drawn symmetric, so their pixel shape tests the aspect ratio). Evidence: `data/derived/<case>/image_track_summary.json`.
- 10/30/2026: GIMBAL glare orientation runs from -51 to +70 deg and matches Sitrec's glare_angle (mean abs difference 0.99 deg after a -0.65 deg offset). Steps at about frames 728, 824, 877, 908, 974 (not 700). At 728, 824, 877 and 974 the glare turns 9 to 30 deg while the cloud background turns 1.7 deg or less (noise 1 to 1.8 deg); at 908 the clouds turn +6.3 deg, matching a +6.3 deg bank change, while the glare turns 24 deg. This rules out a whole-image rotation at the steps. It does not by itself separate a pod-optics glare from a physically rotating object: that test is whether the step times and sizes follow the pod-roll model (M2/M3). Background motion: about 6.4 px/frame early, 1.3 px/frame late (consistent with the decreasing cloud speed both prior analyses use).
- 10/30/2026: GIMBAL apparent size, BLK segment (same polarity): about 235 px at f500, 232 at f700, 210 at f900, 197 at f1030 (low threshold). Within one polarity the blob shrinks by about 16 percent over the second half, which is the opposite of the 15 percent growth reported by Peings and von Rennenkampff. Status: needs care (threshold, polarity, gain changes); M3 size criterion. Evidence: `data/derived/gimbal/image_track.csv`.

- 10/31/2026: GIMBAL range curve hinges on the jet's turn rate (M2.6 first look, placeholders heading 315 and wind 120 kt from 270). With bank from our horizon-bar measurement, a constant-velocity object fits best at long range (misfit falls to the 60 nmi grid edge; flat beyond about 20 nmi at the whole-degree rounding floor) and implies about 906 kt at 30 nmi; inside 20 nmi the fit is poor (1.2 deg RMS at 8 nmi, implied 749 kt). With bank scaled down 8.5 percent to Sitrec's values the curve is flat everywhere and the implied speed at 30 nmi is about 232 kt (and about 4,000 kt at 8 nmi). Elevation within its -2.5 to -1.5 interval moves speeds under 3 kt but altitude by about 575 ft at 8 nmi and 1,300 ft at 30 nmi. Evidence: `data/derived/gimbal/families/summary.json`, `docs/reproductions/gimbal_range_curves.png`. Status: the bank scale must be settled before any GIMBAL conclusion.
- 10/31/2026: Pixel aspect test. The display's center reticle ring (drawn round) measures 6.64 x 6.33 px (GIMBAL, ratio 1.050) and 6.58 x 6.35 px (GOFAST, 1.036) in semi-axes, from radial profiles at angles clear of the bar and tick over 26 frames each. Our frames are therefore about 4 to 5 percent wider than true. That makes measured slopes flatter, so correcting it would RAISE our measured bank by about 4 percent at small angles, not lower it toward Sitrec's. Pixel aspect does not explain the gap; it widens it. Next: an independent turn rate from cloud motion (Sitrec's own default for GIMBAL), and a check of how Sitrec's bank column was measured.
- 10/31/2026: GIMBAL turn rate settled by the lab's own cloud measurement (`docs/reproductions/gimbal_turn_rate.md`). The measured cloud speed falls smoothly from 6.4 to 1.1 px/frame. The cloud-derived turn-rate history matches the timing of Sitrec's hand-keyed cloud curve (RMS difference 0.029 deg/s, 84 percent of frames inside the 95 percent band) and not the coordinated-turn rate from our horizon-bar bank (RMS 0.106 deg/s, 47 percent); total heading change -64.96 deg (95 percent -65.88 to -64.52), closest to the bank-derived total from Sitrec's bank column (-64.99). The horizon bars appear to lag the actual roll by 12 to 54 frames (display filtering suspected); our bank's magnitude is about 3 percent high, or the air was about 9.5 K warmer than ISA (ERA5 decides). Consequences with the cloud-derived rate: the P&vR close path's minimum ground speed falls at f650 to f660 (published f720); a straight, level 340 kt distant path near 32.0 to 32.7 nmi exists in all 40 sampled histories (258 to 295 kt ground speed); the constant-velocity range curve's minimum moves to 27 to 33 nmi. Both the close (stop and reverse) and distant (steady flight) readings are geometrically viable; the video geometry alone does not decide between them. Status: M3 discriminators.
