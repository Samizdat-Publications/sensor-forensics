## Accomplished

- Six hypotheses as data (`solve/hypotheses/*.yaml`): distant aircraft (H-AC), balloon (H-BAL), slow mover (H-SLOW), close small object (H-CLOSE), object on a free path (H-WIT), sensor artifact / glare (H-ART), each with cited priors and the same 0.5 to 60 nmi range support (H-CLOSE is defined by range).
- One scoring path (`solve/score.py`) per the design written before scoring (`docs/design/scoring.md`): interval likelihoods for whole-degree readouts, effective sample sizes, a BIC penalty for every hypothesis, and two totals always side by side (testimony-weighted at D-011's high weight, and physics-only).
- 160 sourced witness claims (`data/testimony/`), 28 scorable terms, applied identically to every hypothesis (`solve/testimony.py`), with a memory floor (D-015) and a separate witness-consistency table.
- Data-quality rules (`docs/results/REVIEW-M3.md`): brightness and size carry weight only when the object is not saturated and is resolved (D-016, D-017). Disclosure (independent review): these rules were NOT decided blind. The brightness audit was triggered after brightness was seen to drive the GIMBAL ranking, and D-017 was ruled after the 0.7 deg ranking with size weighted had been seen; all three exclusions move the GIMBAL physics-only leader to the balloon model. The tests behind them (saturation, resolution) are measurable facts, but results are shown both with and without them in the next revision.
- Results for GOFAST, GIMBAL (both fields of view) and FLIR1; a model-recovery test per video; discriminators.

## What the results say (plain words; SENT BACK by the independent check, see artifacts/review/CP-04-review.md)

- **GOFAST:** the object is at about 13,200 ft (the wind test against the slow flier works one way only). A balloon, a slow flier and a glare on a slow target all fit the same path, and the recovery test shows this data cannot tell them apart. An independent wind measurement at 13,200 ft good to about 4 kt would separate a balloon from an object moving under its own power. The witness memory of an object near sea level does not match the on-screen range and angles.
- **GIMBAL:** on the physical evidence alone, a balloon-like drifter fits best at both fields of view; with testimony weighted high, the object on a free path (consistent with the aircrew's description) leads. But the recovery test shows the balloon, the slow flier and the free path cannot be told apart with this data, so neither ranking is a discrimination; and the testimony-weighted lead depends on one claim term (G-T25, reversal radius) that is scored with a placeholder value for paths that never reverse. The rotation separates a glare in the optics from a physical object, and the measured rotation does not follow the smooth glare model's prediction. At the 0.35 deg field of view, every physical model needs the object at least 15 nmi away; a radar range of 10 nmi or less, to about 2 nmi, would rule all four out, and the aircrew report 6 to 8 nmi. At 0.7 deg this does not hold (the balloon fits at 9 to 13.5 nmi). The rotation result depends on the assumed rotation noise (0.88 deg); the review estimates the glare model would win that criterion above about 1.3 deg, so it is under rerun. That radar data is the decisive missing measurement.
- **FLIR1:** the free-path object leads both totals; the glare models lose on rotation (a balloon, outside FLIR1's design set, also outscores both glare models); testimony pulls the best fits to 6 to 10 nmi. No range is on screen (the range line is a constant 99.9 placeholder).
