## Accomplished

- Six hypotheses as data (`solve/hypotheses/*.yaml`): distant aircraft (H-AC), balloon (H-BAL), slow mover (H-SLOW), close small object (H-CLOSE), object on a free path (H-WIT), sensor artifact / glare (H-ART), each with cited priors and the same 0.5 to 60 nmi range support (H-CLOSE is defined by range).
- One scoring path (`solve/score.py`) per the design written before scoring (`docs/design/scoring.md`): interval likelihoods for whole-degree readouts, effective sample sizes, a BIC penalty for every hypothesis, and two totals always side by side (testimony-weighted at D-011's high weight, and physics-only).
- 160 sourced witness claims (`data/testimony/`), 24 of them scorable, giving 29 scored terms (GIMBAL 9, GOFAST 2, FLIR1 18), applied identically to every hypothesis (`solve/testimony.py`), with a memory floor (D-015) and a separate witness-consistency table.
- Data-quality rules (`docs/results/REVIEW-M3.md`): brightness and size carry weight only when the object is not saturated and is resolved (Rule B, D-016, D-017). **These rules were NOT decided blind.** D-017 was an owner ruling made with both 0.7 deg rankings visible, and the brightness and size reviews were prompted by provisional rankings. So every result is now reported both with and without them (D-018, Rule X), each a full refit.
- Changes after the independent check (D-018; methods in `docs/results/REVIEW-M3.md` section 7). The methods were written before the reruns, but git cannot confirm that order, because section 7, D-018 and the results were committed together (69b5938). The rules were requested by the review, not chosen by the lab, so they are not offered as a verified pre-registration:
  - **Rule X:** totals with and without the exclusions, side by side in every results page and in `results.json` (`no_exclusions`), with a sentence on which ranks change.
  - **Rule N:** the rotation noise is measured from the data only (two-threshold disagreement of the same blob, floored by the frame-to-frame scatter): 0.68 deg at GIMBAL (the earlier 0.88 deg running-median value was larger), 11.9 deg at FLIR1 (earlier 7.5). A sweep on each page brackets the case's own sigma by one rule for every case (0.88, 1.3, 2.0 and 3.0 deg, 0.5 to 2 times the case sigma, and the held-out residual RMS values), with the sigma where each leader changes.
  - **Rule C:** the rotation criterion's free terms (any model's) are scored by blocked cross-validation instead of being counted in the BIC k. BIC was invalid there (H-WIT had k = 49 against a BIC n of 45; physical models had 33 rotation terms against a rotation n_eff of 23). Every k is now below n (H-WIT 16).
  - **Rule G:** the G-T25 reversal-radius term is conditional on a reversal. The 1,000,000 ft sentinel is gone, and G-T11 alone scores whether the object reversed.
  - **Rule K:** per-hypothesis curves over range with ERA5 winds for all three videos.
- Results for GOFAST, GIMBAL (both fields of view) and FLIR1; a model-recovery test per video; discriminators.

## What changed, honestly

- **GIMBAL testimony-weighted leader:** H-WIT before, H-BAL now. The old H-WIT lead rested on G-T25's sentinel (24.6 log units against every path that did not reverse); with the term conditional, H-BAL leads at both fields of view (by 6.2 at 0.35 deg, 0.3 over H-ART at 0.7 deg).
- **The rotation no longer separates glare from a physical object at GIMBAL.** Scored on held-out blocks, the glare derotation predicts the measured rotation slightly better than a free smooth rotation (by 3.5 log units at 0.35 deg, 5.0 at 0.7 deg; held-out RMS 3.6 against 3.7 deg). The earlier 72-unit physical advantage came from the in-sample fit; the earlier glare disadvantage came from the BIC charge on the other side. Rescored over a sweep that now reaches the held-out residual RMS, the physics-only leader is H-BAL at every sigma from about 0.54 deg up to 3.7 deg at both fields of view; below about 0.53 deg a glare model (H-AC or H-ART) leads, and at 0.7 deg the testimony-weighted leader changes to H-ART at 0.66 deg, just below the data-only 0.68 deg. H-SLOW and both glare models are within 4.7 log units of H-BAL at 0.35 deg.
- **Recovery at GIMBAL:** before, the rotation split glare data from physical data every time; now glare-made data are won by the balloon model in 4 of 6 sets at 0.35 deg and 2 of 6 at 0.7 deg. At FLIR1 the split still holds (glare data won by a glare model, physical by physical, apart from one H-CLOSE set won by H-WIT).
- **The exclusions decide the GIMBAL ranking.** Without them H-WIT leads both totals at both fields of view, H-ART and H-AC follow, and H-BAL drops to fifth. With them H-BAL leads. At GOFAST, without the size exclusion the glare model (H-ART) leads instead of the balloon.
- **FLIR1:** unchanged leader (H-WIT) at the data-only sigma, but only just: the leader depends on the rotation sigma (see the second-check list below). The glare models trail by 34 to 38 (physics-only), down from about 360, because the data-derived rotation noise is larger (11.9 deg) and the free rotation is cross-validated. The rotation still favors a physical object by about 268 on held-out data. The glare fits sit at the jet-pitch prior's lower bound (0 deg); lifting it gains them about 100 to 110 log units on rotation, at a pitch far below the prior (`tools/pitch_check.py`, reported on the FLIR1 page).

## What the results say (plain words)

- **GOFAST:** the on-screen range puts the object near 13,200 ft and about 3.8 nmi for every top hypothesis. A balloon, a slow flier and a glare on a slow target fit the same path, and the recovery test shows this data cannot tell them apart; the balloon leads with the size exclusion, the glare model without it. The per-hypothesis range curve, now sampled at 3.5, 3.7, 3.85, 4.0 and 4.5 nmi, peaks at 3.85 nmi for every top hypothesis and falls by hundreds to thousands within a few tenths of a nautical mile; because the range is read on screen, it confirms where the object was but does not separate the hypotheses. An independent wind at 13,200 ft good to about 4 kt would separate a balloon from the free path both ways, but against the slow flier only one way. The relayed case-card report (F-T01, not firsthand) of an object near the sea does not match the on-screen range and angles.
- **GIMBAL:** with the exclusions, a balloon-like drifter leads both totals at both fields of view, but by small margins over the slow flier and the glare models. The rerun recovery test (`docs/results/recovery.md`) shows this data does not separate them: data made by the free path were always won by the balloon at both fields of view, data made by the slow flier mostly so, and now even data made by a glare model were won by the balloon in 4 of 6 sets at 0.35 deg and 2 of 6 at 0.7 deg. Without the exclusions the free path leads. So the GIMBAL ranking is not a discrimination, and it depends on a data-quality choice that was not made blind. The rotation, scored on held-out data, does not separate a glare in the optics from a physical object. The best fits put every physical model at about 12 nmi or more (testimony-weighted: 11.8 to 20.6 nmi at 0.7 deg, 13.8 to 21.2 nmi at 0.35 deg, the nearest being H-WIT at 0.35 deg and H-BAL at 0.7 deg; H-CLOSE, defined as close, excepted); the aircrew report 6 to 8 nmi. Radar range is the decisive missing measurement.
- **FLIR1:** at the data-only rotation sigma (11.86 deg) the free-path object leads both totals and the glare models lose on rotation; testimony pulls the best fits to 8 to 10 nmi. That result is conditional on the sigma. Rescored, the leader changes to the glare model H-ART at 12.75 deg (physics-only) and 12.96 deg (testimony-weighted); refitted at 13.0 and 15.65 deg, H-ART leads both totals (by 77.6 physics-only over H-WIT at 15.65 deg). The sigma itself is doubtful here: the two-threshold estimate (11.86 deg) is larger than the physical fit's whole held-out residual RMS (8.4 deg), which measurement noise cannot be, so the assumption that the two thresholds' errors are equal and independent probably fails. Between 8.4 and 15.65 deg (the physical and glare held-out RMS) the leader changes, so FLIR1 does not decide between a physical object on a free path and a glare in the optics. No range is on screen (the range line is a constant 99.9 placeholder).

## Known issues / debt

- GIMBAL distant-path existence depends on the 0.35 deg field of view (CP-03).
- Speeds at a fixed range are ill-conditioned with whole-degree readouts.
- BIC counts parameters, not prior width: a wide prior (H-ART's 0 to 600 kt) costs nothing extra.
- Range-curve points use fewer random starts than the main fits, so some points (H-WIT's testimony-weighted curve) are jagged.
- The sigma sweep is rescored at each fit's best parameters, not refitted; only FLIR1 has refits (13.0 and 15.65 deg, `data/derived/flir1_navy/sigma_refit.json`).
- Optimizer shortfall: some range-curve points beat the main fits although they use fewer starts (FLIR1: H-AC by 2.6, H-WIT by 1.5; GOFAST: H-CLOSE by about 2,309 at its 2 nmi bound). No leader changes when the better value is taken; each page lists them.
- Recovery was rerun with the earlier generator parameter sets (`--reuse-sets`) and one noise draw per set.
- In the rerun discriminators, H-WIT's range bands come out degenerate (0 nmi); not investigated, not used in any conclusion.
