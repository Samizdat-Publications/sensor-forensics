## What the results say (plain words, provisional until the check)

- **GOFAST:** the object is at about 13,200 ft. A balloon, a slow flier and a glare on a slow target all fit the same path, and the recovery test shows this data cannot tell them apart. An independent wind measurement at 13,200 ft good to about 4 kt would separate a balloon from an object moving under its own power. The witness memory of an object near sea level does not match the on-screen range and angles.
- **GIMBAL:** on the physical evidence alone, a balloon-like drifter fits best at both fields of view; with testimony weighted high, the object on a free path (consistent with the aircrew's description) leads. The rotation separates a glare in the optics from a physical object, and the measured rotation does not follow the smooth glare model's prediction. Every physical model needs the object at least 15 nmi away (at 0.35 deg); a radar range of 10 nmi or less, to about 2 nmi, would rule all four out, and the aircrew report 6 to 8 nmi. That radar data is the decisive missing measurement.
- **FLIR1:** the free-path object leads both totals; the glare models lose on rotation; testimony pulls the best fits to 6 to 10 nmi. No range is on screen (the range line is a constant 99.9 placeholder).
