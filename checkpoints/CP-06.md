- Findings that change how these clips can be read:
  - The tracker-gate labels on the five gate clips (for example "23M") are the gate's **width in metres**, not a range: the label follows the gate size when the gate resizes at fixed zoom (PR044: 3 labelled sizes, the decisive step 33M to 12M as the gate snaps from 304 to 108 px; PR059 slope 0.99 over 5.2x; PR052 supports it by eye, slope 0.75).
  - PR111's label behaves like **slant range** to a level plane, not a gate size, which gives heights below the sensor for two objects.
  - PR113's burned-in scale is a **HUD pitch ladder**, not a depression scale. With square pixels the ladder gives 36.1 px per degree and a horizontal field of view of 51 deg, close to Haqq-Misra and Kopparapu's 54 deg (their 1920 px frame against our 1850 px active width). The image is probably not square-pixelled: a fitted pixel aspect of 1.31 gives 39 deg and a roughly 1.8 aspect by eye gives 28 deg, with the object's angular rate 12 to 20 percent lower. Both assume the ladder is conformal, and the aspect is unresolved.
  - PR149 reproduces Haqq-Misra and Kopparapu's bound: 98 to 130 m/s across the line of sight for a 150 to 200 m ship at the ship's range (theirs 99 to 132). Over frames 15 to 89 the object is below the ship's waterline in the image, so for an object above the sea that is a maximum of the cross-line-of-sight component (the last 8 frames depend on whether the waterline's tilt is camera roll).

## What the results say (plain words)

No PURSUE clip can be reconstructed in 3D: no clip shows the pod azimuth, and most military clips also hide elevation, altitude and range. What remains are sizes and speeds as functions of an unknown range. The tracker labels, read correctly as gate widths, give the object's size and cross-range speed at the range the tracker used (for example PR044: an object at most 0.7 to 1.2 m across, since a few-pixel speck may be unresolved, moving about 1 to 14 m/s across the line of sight at the gate's range, a low-confidence speed from jumpy detections); off that range both scale in proportion. The two clips the 2026 paper analyzed hold up in part: PR149's speed bound is reproduced. PR113's scale is a pitch ladder rather than a depression scale; with square pixels it gives nearly the paper's field of view (51 against 54 deg), but the image is probably stretched horizontally, which would make the field of view narrower (39 or 28 deg) and the angular rate 12 to 20 percent slower. That rests on an unresolved pixel aspect and a conformal ladder.

## Known issues / debt

- PR111 segment C is marginal against the whole-degree rounding, and segment B is a weak test (4 samples).
- PR113's pixel aspect: two indicators disagree (1.31 from the ladder fit over rolls up to 14 deg, about 1.8 from the round reticle by eye).
- The field of view of the gate clips' sensors is unpublished, so the gate's own range is a curve over the assumed field of view.
- PR078 and PR089 never resize their gate at fixed zoom, so the width reading is applied to them from the other gate clips, not tested on them.
- Speeds are the component across the line of sight, relative to the platform: lower bounds on the full relative speed.
